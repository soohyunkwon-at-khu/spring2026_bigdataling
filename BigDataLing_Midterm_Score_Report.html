Distribution Analysis — Histogram & Density Plot

The chart below overlays three elements: (1) a colour-coded histogram of raw counts, (2) a kernel density estimate (KDE) — a smoothed curve showing the probability density of scores — and (3) a dashed normal distribution curve fitted to the same mean and SD, shown as a reference.

Figure 1. Histogram with kernel density estimate (KDE) and normal reference curve. Vertical lines mark the mean (navy, 62.3) and median (teal, 64.0). Bars are colour-coded by score band.

Reading the density plot

  • KDE vs. normal curve: The KDE (solid navy line) departs from the normal reference (dashed grey) with a slight excess of scores in the 50–70 band and a secondary cluster at 80–91, suggesting a mild bimodal tendency visible in the histogram bars.
  • Left skew: The KDE tail extends slightly further left than right (skewness = −0.31). The mean (62.3) sits marginally below the median (64.0), consistent with a mild pull from lower-scoring observations.
  • Platykurtic shape: Excess kurtosis of −0.85 means the distribution is flatter and more spread out than a normal curve — scores are distributed broadly across the full range rather than concentrated near the centre.
  • Normality verdict: Both z-scores (skewness: −0.87; kurtosis: −1.19) fall within ±1.96. The distribution is approximately normal — parametric tests are appropriate.

06

Key Observations

📊 Score spread
The largest single band is 60–69 (11 students, 23.4%). The 50–59 band is the second largest (9 students, 19.1%), meaning a substantial portion of the class scored in the upper half of this range — closer to 60 than to 50.
📈 Polarised distribution
The IQR of 28 points (Q1 = 52, Q3 = 80) and SD of 18.9 indicate a broadly spread distribution. There appear to be two informal clusters: one around 50–70 and another around 80–91. This bimodal tendency is visible in the density plot.
✅ Approximately normal
Both standardised skewness (−0.87) and kurtosis (−1.19) are within ±1.96. Parametric statistical tests (t-tests, ANOVA, regression) are appropriate for this dataset.
